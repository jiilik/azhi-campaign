**Status: PROPOSED** (opened 2026-10-31). This is the working record for Azhi's deep history. Troy's statements are marked *(Troy)*; nothing here is canon until he approves it.

## 1. Troy's dump (2026-10-31)

- **The myth stands.** *(Troy)* `azhi-myth.md` and the `.rtf` are still canonical. But much may have happened between the legend and the present day of play: a **significant amount of time**, even **geological** amounts, given the erosion and landscape changes on the map since Azhi's death.
- **Eras with epic shake-ups** *(Troy)*: like the major eras in Final Fantasy XIV, each ending in a shake-up of the whole civilization.
  - Build a **deep timeline** of these shake-ups, with **geography** to match: ruins of ancient cities, former dragon lairs and so on.
- **Six epochs before the present** *(Troy)*, each ending in some catastrophe:
  1. The first is **Azhi eating the original planet**.
  2. The second starts at the **formation of this world**.
  3. *Etc.:* details to be filled in later.
- **No more hybrids or colour mixing** *(Troy, planned)*: remove every reference to hybrids, blends and colour mixing, and write **new lore explaining how the other colours emerged**.
- **Yellow and Grey** *(Troy)*:
  - In one epoch, the **Black dragon** tried to create new adult dragons, **Yellow and Grey**, under its direct control: **homunculi** of the true Gold and Silver dragons, who largely don't care.
  - It was an attempt to **subvert the few Gold and Silver dragonborn** on the world by forcing them under a new hierarchy.
  - **It worked:** every Gold and Silver dragonborn of that era was converted to Yellow or Grey.
  - The **other dragons aligned to kill the Yellow and Grey homunculi** in an epic conclusion, in the style of *The Lord of the Rings*.
    - *Changed by Troy, 2026-11-01:* **the dragonborn killed the homunculi**, with *Wish*. See the session notes for 2026-11-01, "spell origins".
  - **Afterwards,** Yellow and Grey dragonborn continue to be born, but they **align with the Gold and Silver dragons**.
  - There are **rumours of true Gold and Silver dragonborn** in later eras, but the information is patchy.
  - **To do:** choose which epoch this happened in. Perhaps the one just before the present.
  - *(Confirmed by Troy: "yellow and silver" was a typo for Yellow and Grey.)*

**Epoch 5: the homunculi war.**
- Ended about **two centuries ago**. Since dragonborn can live on by phasing, there are dragonborn **who remember it**: veterans, perhaps in a different life and colour.

- **Parked:** whether today's Yellow and Grey dragonborn carry a **stigma** from the war.

**Epoch 4: the twins, and the origin of Brown and Indigo.**
1. **The tryst:** Blue and Green, neighbours on the wheel and the two dragons who get along best, have a tryst. **Two dragon eggs** result, the first eggs any of the dragons has ever seen.
2. **Unphased twins:** the eggs hatch into **Unphased dragons**. Everyone expects them to settle as Blue and Green.
3. **The others interfere:** they fear two Blues and two Greens would unbalance the five worldly dragons.
   - **Black** tries to sway a twin to Black, because more Black is better.
   - **White** tries too, defensively, for fear that Black will succeed.
4. **Black succeeds:** one twin phases **Black**, making two Black dragons. The rift between the twins, and the others' fear of two Blacks, makes the other twin phase **Red**.
   - The new Red is a **different, militant shade**, perhaps crimson. The elder Red is industrial, a maker rather than a warrior.
5. **The siblings' war:** young Black and young Red fight an enormous war.
   - **Mass phasing to raise armies:** dragonborn phase in great numbers into the young Red's shade to fight, and young Black convinces many dragonborn to phase into its own black.
   - **Weapons:** Black, being clever, makes its own weapons. Nobody had to sell them. Some were so dangerous that the elder Black later took the knowledge with it (6).
   - *(Troy)* Not every epoch should be everyone against Black, which would be repetitive. Here it's a war between siblings.
6. **The war ends** when the **elder Black** realizes the disaster is partly its fault and takes responsibility.
   - It phases into **Indigo**, becoming the elder Indigo dragon. The young Black is left as *the* Black dragon.
   - It **takes the knowledge and secrets of the war's worst weapons with it**. *(Claude)* That fits Indigo dealing in secrets and forbidden things, and gives an adventure hook if those secrets ever surface.
7. **The young Red phases into Brown,** against expectations; everyone thought Blue or Green, or something in between. It's a **swords-into-ploughshares** change: from now on, rest, humility, humbleness and peace instead of war.
8. **The first phasing of adult dragons:** epoch 4 is the first time fully grown, god-like dragons ever changed colour.
9. **The council:** afterwards, the dragons form **something like a council of the gods**, to stop mass phasing to raise armies from ever happening again.

**Consequences for the present (epoch 6):**
- **Today's Black dragon is not the original.** It's the young Black from epoch 4, still trying to wrest power from the others, which is why it made the homunculi. A ready-made political rivalry for the current era.
- **The council fell apart** after the homunculi war. What's left is **informal**, with no grand name: an association of **Green, White and Indigo** who keep meeting.
  - **Stays out:** **Brown** (true neutrality), plus **Red** and **Blue** (commerce; they sell to all comers).
  - **Absconded:** young Black.
  - **Above it all:** Gold and Silver, aloof as ever.
- *(Claude)* Mass phasing ties into the phasing brainstorm (`canon/brainstorm-phasing.md`), as both a weapon of war and something the gods have banned.

**Parked:** the map (no geography yet), the Yellow and Grey stigma, and what killing the homunculi released.

**Epoch 2: the machine war** (magic against technology).
- *(Troy)* D&D and Final Fantasy disasters are often about magic against technology. It's a way to scatter **technological artifacts** around a world that's largely magical today: there was a big conflict, and magic came out on top.

If so, epoch 2 isn't their invention but a **return**. *(Claude)* The myth says everything Azhi ate flowed back out of its wounds, so first-world machines could have come out of the corpse as half-understood relics.
- **Alignments** (before any council or peace):
  - **For the machines:** **Red and Blue**. *(Claude)* Red builds; Blue's ships and ports trade.
  - **Against:**
    - **Green**, for ecological reasons.
    - **Black**, but **for selfish reasons**. *(Claude)* Black gave mortals magic "so that we may be worshipped", so machines that work without magic threaten the worship.
    - **White**, the colour most likely to reject technology outright. It's the ascetics in their monasteries, preserving the status quo.
- **The end: a singularity.**
  - The machines wake. *(Claude)* A thinking engine outgrows its makers and inherits **Azhi's hunger**, consuming the land to build more of itself. That's the **ecological catastrophe**.
  - **Red and Blue flip** to fight their own creation, so all five dragons fight together.
  - The dragons **convince it to eat itself**, beating it with Azhi's own end.
- **Aftermath:**
  - **Lots of little machine fragments are scattered all over the world,** largely **forbidden**.
  - One of **White's monastic missions** is to track these artifacts down and remove them from circulation. *(Claude)* That gives White adventurers a job, and smugglers a profit.

**Epoch 3: the age of faith.**
- **The rise of religion** as a concept takes over the whole civilization, with **wars between religions**. It's not only Black seeking followers.
  - **Blue's religious hucksters** create competing religions: a **bazaar of ideas**.
  - The churches **aren't necessarily run by the dragons**, except perhaps the Black church.
- **The end: a magical apocalypse.**
  - Dragonborn worshippers learn to **tap the dragons' own power through their worship**. The churches overreach, and the result is a magical apocalypse.
  - It **hurt the dragons a little**: **their powers today are less than at the creation of the world**.
  - The dragons step in to **turn off the taps**, so their dragonborn can't draw that power again.
- **The 9th-level cap** *(Troy: "I think this is going to be a rule")*:
  - The churches reached spells **above 9th level** by drawing on the dragons.
  - Afterwards, all spells above 9th level were **blocked**. This gives the game's cap an in-world reason.
  - It borrows the concept only from D&D's Karsus's Folly in Netheril, where a mortal briefly became a god and magic was capped. None of its wording is used.

### 2026-10-31, voice, afternoon drive: review, stigma, monsters, relics

Everything here is Troy's, unless marked *(Claude)*. Troy reviewed the outline epoch by epoch and approved it, with the additions below.

**Magic after epoch 3.** "Turning off the taps" is a metaphor: magic wasn't switched off. As with Karsus's Folly, magic was damaged, then recovered over time. Clerics get their divine magic as usual; only spells above 9th level stay sealed.

**The Yellow and Grey stigma.**
- The ancestors of today's Yellow and Grey were **lured into phasing by false gods**.
- Because dragonborn phase in and out, today's Yellow and Grey are mostly not the same people. So the stigma is **not personal**: it's a sense that the colours themselves may be **unnatural**.
- The prejudice shows up **in cultures and religions, not between individuals**.
- **Near-misses:** someone who phases into Yellow or Grey today was probably **trying to reach Gold or Silver, and fell short**. That makes them a little wistful.
- It gives the DM an in-world answer when a player asks to become Gold: reach for Gold and you'll most likely land on Yellow, the stand-in.
- **Yellow and Grey are recent.** They can be abundant today, but when old dragons or old dragonborn talk about the old times, they describe **a world with no Yellow or Grey**, though with **infrequent Gold and Silver** dragonborn.
- **Gold and Silver today** are only **whispered about**. Not a secret in the Indigo sense: the aim is that meeting a real Gold or Silver in play feels like meeting a living myth. This fits keeping their rules in the DM material.

**Monstrosities: what killing the homunculi released.**
- *(Troy)* The world needs an in-world reason for the monstrosities the players fight.
- *(Claude, accepted)* The homunculi were artificial, so their power had nowhere to return when they died. It **spilled out and twisted the creatures around them**.
- **Blame falls everywhere,** and much of it is misplaced. People blame:
  - the Black dragon;
  - the dragons who killed the homunculi;
  - the true Gold and Silver, absent while their image was stolen;
  - whoever else is handy.
- Most people **don't know today's Black is the young Black**, not the ancient one. The young Black has done a **really good job on propaganda**, pushing the blame onto everyone but itself. Plenty of politics, intrigue and whispered secrets.
- To return to in the monsters chapter.

**The ancient Black dragon was a hero, twice.**
- **In the machine war:** thinking machines threatened its gift of thought to the world, so defeating them is part of its heroic legacy. That's alongside the selfish motive of worship.
- **In the War of the Twins:** it's possibly the real hero, by taking responsibility and becoming Indigo, though that's less well known today.

**Crimson** *(DM option)*:
- A **very rare** Crimson dragonborn, phasing toward the memory of the old war-Red, can be a DM option, like Gold and Silver.
- At campaign scale: if the world goes badly enough in this epoch, someone might try to **convince the Brown dragon to phase back to Crimson**.

**Myths for each epoch** *(to do, written by Troy)*:
- Troy will write a canonical myth for each epoch himself, so it doesn't read like LLM writing.
- **The further back the epoch, the less reliable its myth:** people dispute the old ones.
- **The homunculi war is in living memory,** so its account must be precise, almost technical, because people can physically dispute it.

**Relics and sites, one per epoch** (big sites, for a player overview):
1. **Epoch 1: Azhi's brain.**

### 2026-11-01, text: thinning boundaries (inspired by *The Laundry Files*)

**The idea** *(Troy)*: as in Charles Stross's *The Laundry Files*, too much computation or magic weakens the boundaries of reality. In the books, magic is a branch of mathematics, and enough of it, from sorcerers or computers, thins the walls between universes until things on the other side can reach through. Concept only; nothing of Stross's wording, names or entities is used.

**Cosmic horror is in** *(Troy)*: things can break through from outside. This gives more variety of monsters and plots.

**How it could tie the epochs together** *(Claude, PROPOSED)*:
- **Epoch 2:** the machine age computed so much that reality thinned. That could explain how the machines built their hiding plane, and why pocket dimensions date from that era.
- **Epoch 3:** the churches drew so much power that the boundaries tore. That's how the elemental planes became reachable.
- **Epoch 6:** the imperfectly sealed rifts are the scars, and **concentrations of magic or computation** are dangerous wherever they gather. Possible sites:
  - the young Black's lair in Azhi's brain;
  - the last living machine;
  - great temples and mage-schools;
  - any future attempt to rebuild the machines.
- **Horror sources:** from outside the world, through the thinned places. Possibly also the void Azhi wandered (the Astral, if that's what the cosmos is).

**Questions it raises:**
- What lies outside? Nameless, or with a lore of its own?
- Does heavy magic use carry a **personal** cost too, as well as the cosmic one?
- Who watches for thin places today? A faction, perhaps White's monasteries, which already hunt machine fragments.
- How does it relate to the monster review? *(Claude)* The SRD's Aberrations (9 monsters: Aboleth, Chuul, Cloaker, Gibbering Mouther and so on) are a natural fit for things from outside.

### 2026-11-01, voice: the origin of magic

Everything here is Troy's, unless marked *(Claude)*.

**The history of spellcasting:**
1. **Epoch 1** (the first world): **no magic except the dragons' own**. The dragons could cast spells **up to level 12**, and **Azhi above that**.
2. **Epoch 2** (the machine war): **the dragons teach the dragonborn to cast spells**, up to level 5, "maybe up to level 9", to fight the machines.
3. **After the machines' defeat,** magic persists in the world, and the dragonborn know how to use it.
4. **Seven sources:** only the five worldly dragons, plus Gold and Silver, existed when magic was taught. So **they are the source of all the magic the dragonborn know**.
5. **To do (deferred):** go through all the spells and assign each one to **the dragon that taught it**. This will add flavour for writing spells later.
   - *(Troy)* **Not yet.** He wants to think through the knock-on effects first.
6. **Epoch 3** (the magical apocalypse): somehow, the **dragonborn cast spells above level 9**. That's the advent of higher-level magic among the dragonborn.
7. **The fix:** the available magic in the whole system had to be reduced. The dragons, the source of it all, **turn off the taps: they drop themselves to level 9 casters**. This prevents the final apocalypse of that epoch.
   - *(Refines the earlier notes: "a little weaker than at creation" becomes a cut from level 12 to level 9.)*
8. **The consequence:** **dragonborn can now match the dragons in spellcasting.** That **reduces the godhood element** of the dragons.
   - They're still major, epic influencers in the world, with a long history and a connection to Azhi at the beginning of the world. But they are also **just dragons**.
9. **The players' crisis:** **the Black dragon is attempting to achieve godhood.** It's the young Black, not the original from the age of god-like dragons, and it covets the power the ancient Black had.

**Knock-on effects and questions** *(Claude)*:
- **The myth, epoch 1:** it says that through Black's gift, life on the first world "learns to control fire, water, earth and ice". That reads like mortal magic **in epoch 1**, which clashes with "no magic except the dragons' own". Options:
  - **a.** That control was something lesser than spellcasting: awareness, or a knack, not spells.
  - **b.** The first world's magic was lost when Azhi ate it, and had to be taught again in epoch 2.
  - **c.** The myth is "not the only telling" and simplifies.
  
  The myth's wording is fixed either way.
- **The epoch 2 cap:** level 5 or level 9? If the dragonborn reached 9th level only in epoch 3, the epoch 3 jump would be from 9 to above 9.
- **The machine war's sides:** Red and Blue backed the machines at first. Did they also teach magic to fight them, after they flipped? Or did only Green, White, Black, Gold and Silver teach?
- **Later colours' magic:** Brown and Indigo (epoch 4), and Yellow and Grey (epoch 5), didn't exist when magic was taught.
  - Indigo carries the ancient Black's knowledge, so Black spells might pass through it.
  - Yellow and Grey are near-misses for Gold and Silver.
  - Assigning spells to dragons will need to say how the newer colours fit.
- **The dragons in the rules:** "level 9 casters" means **the god-dragons need their own stat blocks**. The SRD's ancient dragons are the closest starting point. A dragonborn matching a dragon in spellcasting doesn't mean matching it in everything else.
- **Godhood means breaking the cap.** The young Black's bid for godhood is a bid to cast above level 9 again. That ties together:
  - **thinning boundaries:** casting above the cap thins reality, which could let things through;
  - **its lair in Azhi's brain:** Azhi was the one caster above level 12;
  - **the epoch 3 disaster:** a warning of where it leads.
- **Earlier wording:** the 2026-10-31 note said "turning off the taps" was only a metaphor. It's now concrete: the dragons capped themselves. Clerics and divine magic still work as before.
