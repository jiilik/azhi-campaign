# Brainstorm: Personality Archetypes

**Status: brainstorming only (PROPOSED).** This is research for reworking the personality traits in `canon/brainstorm-species.md` §9 and `srd/04-character-origins/01-personality-traits.md`. Nothing here changes canon until Troy approves it.

Sources: voice sessions and research, 2026-10-22.

---

## 1. The goal

- *(Troy)* The current traits are too much like feats. Keep traits separate from mechanics, and **start from personalities and work forward**, rather than from the six abilities backward.
- *(Troy)* A player should be able to build a character whose personality they'd actually play at the table, rather than picking a bonus and finding out they're a glutton.
- *(Troy)* The traits still need mechanics, because they replace backgrounds and Origin feats in character building.
- *(Troy)* The models below are **inspiration only**. Their names and systems won't be published, so a shared word here and there is fine.

## 2. What each model offers

| Model | What it sorts | What it adds for us | Gaps or cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Myers-Briggs** (16 types) | Four either/or axes: introvert/extravert, intuitive/sensing, thinking/feeling, judging/perceiving | A familiar, complete spread of 16 everyday personalities | Every type is flattering. It has no axis for emotional stability, and its either/or splits don't match how people actually vary. Its trademark means we use it for inspiration only. |
| **Big Five** (OCEAN) and its facets | Five scales, each with six facets | **Neuroticism** fills Myers-Briggs's biggest gap, and the facets name concrete behaviours | It's built on scales, not types, so it needs turning into archetypes |
| **HEXACO** | The Big Five plus **Honesty-Humility** | The scheming, greedy and arrogant end of personality | That end is dark, so pick with care |
| **Dark Triad / Tetrad** | Machiavellianism, narcissism, psychopathy, plus everyday sadism | Clear villain archetypes | Mostly NPC material. The *Light Triad* is its hopeful counterpart. |
| **Enneagram** (9 types) | What drives someone and what they fear | **Motivation**, which hooks straight into roleplay | It overlaps with the other models on behaviour |
| **Four temperaments** | Sanguine, choleric, melancholic, phlegmatic | Ancient, public-domain and already elemental (air, fire, earth, water) | Traits are meant to stay independent of colours, so don't map these onto the wheel |
| **Pendragon** (RPG) | 13 virtue/vice pairs, such as Valorous/Cowardly and Prudent/Reckless | Opposed pairs, plus a roll that can make a character act on a trait against the player's wish | Its full system is heavier than ours |
| **Fate** (RPG) | Aspects that can be *compelled* | You accept a complication from your own aspect and earn a fate point | Its point economy is separate from D&D's |

### Details worth keeping

- **Big Five facets** *(Wikipedia, NEO PI-R)*:
  - Neuroticism: anxiety, hostility, depression, self-consciousness, impulsiveness, vulnerability.
  - Extraversion: warmth, gregariousness, assertiveness, activity, excitement-seeking, positive emotions.
  - Openness: fantasy, aesthetics, feelings, actions, ideas, values.
  - Agreeableness: trust, straightforwardness, altruism, compliance, modesty, tender-mindedness.
  - Conscientiousness: competence, order, dutifulness, achievement striving, self-discipline, deliberation.
- **Honesty-Humility facets** *(HEXACO)*: sincerity, fairness, greed avoidance, modesty. People who score low on these are the Dark Triad types.
- **Enneagram drives:**

| Type | Wants | Fears |
|---|---|---|
| Reformer | Integrity | Being bad |
| Helper | To be loved | Being unlovable |
| Achiever | To be valued | Being worthless |
| Individualist | To be themselves | Having no identity |
| Investigator | Mastery | Helplessness |
| Loyalist | Security | Being unsupported |
| Enthusiast | Freedom | Being trapped or in pain |
| Challenger | Control | Being dominated |
| Peacemaker | Peace | Conflict and disconnection |

- **Four temperaments and Eysenck:**
  - Sanguine: outgoing and steady.
  - Choleric: outgoing and volatile.
  - Melancholic: inward and volatile.
  - Phlegmatic: inward and steady.
  - Wikipedia flags this ancient-to-modern mapping as poorly sourced.
- **Pendragon's 13 pairs:**
  - Chaste/Lustful, Energetic/Lazy, Forgiving/Vengeful, Generous/Selfish, Honest/Deceitful.
  - Just/Arbitrary, Merciful/Cruel, Modest/Proud, Pious/Worldly, Prudent/Reckless.
  - Temperate/Indulgent, Trusting/Suspicious, Valorous/Cowardly.
- **SRD 5.2.1, already in the rules:**
  - GMs typically award Heroic Inspiration "when you do something particularly heroic, in character, or entertaining."
  - You can only hold one Heroic Inspiration at a time; a spare can be given to another player character.
  - Source: `srd/01-playing-the-game/03-d20-tests.md`.

## 6. Design ideas from the research (all PROPOSED, Claude)

1. **Every trait has an upside and a pull.** The upside is a small, situational benefit. The *pull* is how the personality gets in the way, as with Pendragon's vices and Fate's compels.
2. **Playing the pull earns Heroic Inspiration.** When the DM (or the player) invokes the pull and the player goes along with it, the character gains Heroic Inspiration. The SRD already gives Heroic Inspiration for in-character play, so this just makes it concrete. The mechanics then come from acting in character rather than from a bonus menu.
3. **Opposed pairs.** Build the list as pairs of opposites (Worrier/Rock, Miser/Generous, Coward/Daredevil). A character can't take both sides. That generalizes the current Light Sleeper/Heavy Sleeper rule and makes the list read as a spectrum.
4. **Loosen the ability link.** The fixed +1 per trait is the "working backwards" part. Options:
   - Each trait suggests two abilities.
   - Each trait suggests a spread, as the SRD's "Creating a Background" does.
   - The +1s move entirely to the affinity.
   This would be a canon change, so it needs Troy's call.
5. **Shadow traits are allowed for players, cruelty isn't.** A Schemer or a Braggart is fun to play; a sadist mostly makes the table uncomfortable.
6. **Size.** There are about 16 everyday archetypes plus about 30 gap archetypes, so roughly 45 in all. Pairing and merging could bring that to 20–24 pairs, near Troy's 12–20 target.

### Positive and negative traits

*(Claude)* The 31 split roughly three ways:

| Lean | Archetypes |
|---|---|
| **Positive** (14) | Strategist, Tinkerer, Commander, Fixer, Seer, Dreamer, Mentor, Spark, Keeper, Guardian, Host, Wanderer, Showman, Rock |
| **Negative** (12) | Worrier, Hothead, Brooder, Schemer, Braggart, Cold Fish, Miser, Cynic, Coward, Grudge-Holder, Hedonist, Lazybones |
| **On the fence** (5) | Perfectionist, Daredevil, Trickster, Rebel, Zealot |

- *(Troy)* Every trait should have a **weakness**: if you're too kind, you can be taken advantage of.
- *(Troy)* Keep positives and negatives **roughly balanced**, though it doesn't have to be even. Fence-sitters either get adjusted so they read clearly one way, or are **split into two traits** with opposite implications.
  - *(Claude)* Examples: the Zealot becomes the **Devout** (positive) and the **Fanatic** (negative); the Rebel becomes the **Free Thinker** and the **Troublemaker**. Splitting all five would give about 19 positives and 17 negatives.
- *(Claude)* Several archetypes have natural opposites, such as the Daredevil and the Coward, or the Host and the Miser.

### Proposed structure

- *(Troy)* A phased character takes **one positive trait and one negative trait**.
- *(Claude, Troy agreed)* An **Unphased** character takes **two positives and one negative**.
- *(Troy)* The reason is table culture. You can't change a player's personality, but you can cap how much negativity their character projects; a negative player with a negative character "can really just stack up really badly."
- *(Claude)* The positive trait carries the mechanical upside. The negative trait is the **pull** (§6): the flaw that earns Heroic Inspiration when the player leans into it.

### Exclusions

- *(Troy)* Some traits can't be taken together, because they cancel each other out or stack in a broken way. For example, a character taking both the Free Thinker and the Rebel side would double up.
- *(Troy)* Show this as an **Exclusion** line on each trait, like a feat's Prerequisite line: "Cannot be taken with A, B or C."
- *(Claude)* The simple rule behind it: you can't take both halves of a split trait, or both sides of an opposed pair.

### Making negatives worth choosing

- *(Troy)* Negative traits still need a **reason to choose them**, or players will take the best positive and the mildest negative. Drawbacks **mustn't be too large**.
- *(Claude, Troy agreed)* Give each flaw **its own small silver lining**; for example, the Hothead's temper also makes them hard to push around. Players then pick the flaw they'd enjoy playing. The Heroic Inspiration it earns counts toward its points.

### Points

*(Claude's split, Troy agreed)*

| Character | Affinity | Positive traits | Negative trait | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phased | ~16 | 1 × ~12 | ~8 | **~36** |
| Unphased | — | 2 × ~12 | ~8 | **~32** |

- *(Troy)* The Unphased being about 4 points behind is a fair trade for their flexibility.
- The current canon budget is "about 35" after skills were repriced (`canon/brainstorm-species.md` §7). This split uses 36. The difference is within rounding, but it should be reconciled if the system is adopted.

### Unphased: no staying Unphased forever

- *(Troy)* Some players will want to stay Unphased for the flexibility. So **each time an Unphased character gains a level, they must pass a check or be forced to phase.**
- *(Claude)* Cautions, not yet decided:
  - A pure roll could force a phase at a bad moment. A DC that rises each level would make phasing steadily more likely.
  - Alternatively, the player chooses whether to make the check or give in and phase.
- **Open:** who picks the new colour, the player or the story.

### Phasing from Unphased: the bridge

- *(Troy)* When an Unphased character phases, they **keep two of their three traits: the negative one and one positive.** They're effectively swapping a positive trait for an elemental affinity, going from about 32 points to about 36.
- *(Troy)* The character should keep a **bridge** between their Unphased and phased selves. *(Claude)* Phasing then feels like growing up rather than a punishment.

### Suggested colours on each trait

- *(Troy)* Under each trait description, list **suggested colour affinities**, covering both the five colours and the five hybrids. Traits then group naturally into a "canonical build" for each form. For example, someone building a White dragonborn can see which traits line up with White.
- *(Claude)* This is guidance, not a rule. Any trait still works with any form, which keeps the canon rule that traits aren't tied to colours, much as the core rules suggest ability scores for each class.

### Stray idea: experience traits instead of classes

- *(Troy, just a thought)* Extend the system into classes: instead of advancing in a class, characters gain **experience traits**.
- *(Claude)* That would be a classless system, like RuneQuest's improvement by doing. Classes hold most of the SRD's balance, so a full replacement would be a huge job. A middle ground: gain an experience trait at the levels where a character would normally take a feat.

### Revised questions for Troy

- Adopt the **one positive, one negative** structure (two positives and one negative for the Unphased)?
- Which of the 31 archetypes to keep, and how to resolve the fence-sitters?
- The **forced-phasing check**: a flat check, a rising DC, or the player's choice? Who picks the colour?
- Reconcile the **36 vs 35** budget, and settle whether the **+1 per trait** stays (§6, idea 4).
- How the **current twelve traits** carry over (§5).

## 9. Four groups: Mind, Heart, Hand and Shadow (2026-10-22, late evening)

### The frame

- *(Troy)* Trait budget is about **20 points** for a phased character and **about 30** for the Unphased. Two traits per character was an arbitrary choice and isn't locked in.
- *(Troy)* New frame: **four groups of traits, each worth about 5 points; pick one from each group.** The Unphased get two extra picks.
- *(Troy)* A cap on stat traits: "can only choose two that boost primary stats."
- *(Claude, Troy preferred it)* Group by what the trait does at the table. **Mind, Heart and Hand** line up with D&D's exploration, social and combat pillars. **Shadow** is the flaw group.
  - Everyone gets something in every pillar and **exactly one flaw**.
  - Colours stay as suggested pairings.
- Considered and set aside:
  - Grouping by alignment: "one from each" would force an evil trait and a lawful-and-chaotic clash.
  - Grouping by elemental colour: it ties traits to colours, though it was very flavourful (an Unphased character takes one trait of every colour).

### The maths *(Claude)*

- 5 points per trait means a trait is either:
  - a **stat trait**: +1 to an ability (4) plus a ribbon-sized perk (about 1); or
  - a **knack**: a perk worth about 5, with no stat.
- **At most two stat traits** (Troy's cap) plus the affinity's +1 gives the usual **+3** to ability scores. Taking a knack instead of a stat trait is an even trade.
- **Unphased:** 4 + 2 = 6 picks, about 30 points. Up to **three** stat traits, since they have no affinity +1. Their two extra picks come from Mind, Heart or Hand, never a second Shadow.
- **Shadow traits** are worth about 5. The silver lining plus the Heroic Inspiration earned by playing the flaw make up the value. *(Proposed:)* Shadow traits carry no +1.
- This replaces the earlier 12/8 split (§8). Phased: 16 + 20 = 36. Unphased: about 30.

The fence-sitters take their positive reading here; their darker halves could become Shadow traits later.

## 10. Positive traits only: the nineteen (voice session, morning of 2026-10-23)

- *(Troy)* We're building a **heroic party of adventurers**, so **points go only to positive traits.**

## 11. Back to two traits (2026-10-23, afternoon)

## 12. Flavour descriptions, one trait at a time (from 2026-10-23)

### The Strategist (draft)

> You see the whole board. Before the first blow lands, you've counted the exits, weighed your enemies and guessed their next three moves, and you're already thinking about the war after this battle. Others call it overthinking; you call it winning.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours Black and Oxblood; Intelligence or Wisdom; a benefit that rewards planning ahead (around Initiative, or the first round of a fight).

### The Theorist (draft)

> Everyone else accepts that the spine of the world never bends. You want to know why. You chase an idea down to its bones, take it apart and put it back together a little better, and you'll happily miss supper doing it. A good question keeps you warmer than any fire.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours Black and Slate; Intelligence; a benefit for working out how something works, or why. Keep it clear of Slate's Bonus Action Study.

### The Commander (draft)

> When the shouting starts, every head turns to you, and you've already decided. You see where the group should be going and can't help pointing the way. You'd rather make a bold call and own it than wait for a perfect one, and somehow people keep following you.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours Red and Oxblood; Charisma or Strength; a benefit that lets allies act on your orders in a crisis. Keep it clear of White's Help as a Bonus Action. The Commander is the party face; the Steward is the second in command.

### The Debater (draft)

> You'll argue that the Gold dragon is just a very bright moth, simply to see who can prove you wrong. Every plan, rule and sacred truth deserves a good poke, and you'll switch sides mid-argument for the fun of it. Nothing delights you more than the rare day someone finally wins one against you.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours Blue and Black; Charisma or Intelligence; a benefit in arguments and negotiations, such as finding the hole in someone's case. Keep it clear of Blue's Trader's Tongue reroll. *(Troy)* You could build a politician from it.

### The Confidant (draft; was the Seer)

*(Troy, tentative)* Renamed **the Confidant**, because "the Seer" implied magic. It comes from INFJ (16Personalities' Advocate, Keirsey's Counselor). Also considered: the Discerning, the Perceptive, the Insightful, the Counsellor, the Wise (too tied to the Wisdom score), the Silent (William the Silent), the Discreet, the Confessor and the Trusted. *(Claude)* With the new name, the line "It isn't magic" could go.

> You notice the pause before an answer, the glance toward the door, the smile that stops short of the eyes. People tell you things they've told no one else, and you often know how a story ends before it's half told. It isn't magic. You simply pay attention.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours White and Slate; Wisdom or Charisma; a benefit for reading intentions and sensing where a situation is heading. Intuition about people, not divination: the Prudent foresees by logic, the Seer by reading people. Keep it clear of Glacier's people-reading.

### The Dreamer (draft)

> Somewhere behind your eyes is a better world, and you can see it in every detail. You hold to what's right even when it costs you, and you'd rather fail at something worth doing than succeed at something hollow. People mistake your quiet for distance. It's conviction.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours White and Glacier; Wisdom or Charisma; a benefit for holding firm to your values under pressure. Keep it clear of Black's Guarded Mind (Advantage on mental saving throws).

### The Mentor (draft)

> You see what someone could become long before they do, and you can't help nudging them toward it. Your praise lands like a gift, and even your hard truths somehow land kindly. Half the people you've met are better for knowing you; the other half just haven't noticed yet.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours White and Slate; Charisma or Wisdom; a benefit that helps an ally grow or do better at something you're good at. Keep it clear of White's Help as a Bonus Action. The Commander rallies people in the moment; the Mentor improves them over time; the Confidant listens.

### The Curious (draft)

> A locked door is a question, and you've never once left a question alone. You read the margins, pull the lever marked "do not pull", and take the left fork, then come back for the right one. Your companions have learned to count heads before leaving a room.

- *Seeds (Claude):* suggested colours Blue and Marsh; Dexterity or Intelligence; a benefit for finding what's hidden: secret doors, side passages, the thing behind the thing. Keep it clear of Slate's Bonus Action Search. The Theorist asks why; the Curious asks what's in there; the Wanderer asks what's over the next hill.
