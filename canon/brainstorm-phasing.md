# Brainstorm: Phasing (and Mortality)

**Status: PROPOSED** (opened 2026-10-30). This is the working record for the phasing rules. Its final home is probably Character Origins ("Settling and Phasing"), with spillover into other chapters (§3). Nothing here is canon until Troy approves it.

## 1. What's already canon

- **Born Unphased:** all dragonborn are born polychromatic (Unphased). They **settle** into a form as they mature, and what triggers settling is left to the player's backstory.
- **Phasing:** something dramatic, traumatic or deadly can make a dragonborn **phase** from one form into another.
  - They keep parts of their former personality and most of their memories.
  - Rebirth runs deep, echoing the myth: everything Azhi devoured "springs forth anew".
  - Phasing **carries a penalty**, so it can't be used for advantage.
- **A New Society:** a phased dragonborn must find a place in their new colour. They stay in their old role, or start again at the bottom.
  - A newly phased Indigo has no name and no pearls, and is soon approached with an opportunity.
- **No Return:** once settled, there's no return to Unphased, except rarely, at the DM's discretion.
- **Languages** don't change when a dragonborn phases.
- **Gold and Silver** aren't easy to reach. They're backburnered for the DM material.
- *(Troy, 2026-10-30)* Since Azhi died and was reborn as a world, the game might remove mortality altogether, after a fashion. **Life span** was taken out of Character Origins to be settled here.

## 2. Open questions

1. **What is phasing, in game terms?** Is it an event the DM triggers, a player's choice, a consequence of death, or some mix?
2. **The trigger:** what counts as "dramatic, traumatic or deadly"? Does dropping to 0 Hit Points count? Does death?
3. **Death and mortality:** does phasing replace death, wholly or partly? Is there still permanent death?
4. **The new form:** is it chosen, rolled, or set by the event? Do neighbours on the wheel matter?
5. **What carries over:**
   - Personality traits (probably kept, since "parts of their former personality" survive).
   - Class levels, proficiencies, equipment, memories and relationships.
6. **The penalty:** what stops a player phasing on purpose to optimize?
7. **First settling:** is it phasing, with the penalty, or free?
8. **Age:** does phasing reset age or appearance?
9. **Frequency:** is there a limit on how often a dragonborn can phase?

## 3. Spillover to check

- **Spells:** *Raise Dead*, *Resurrection*, *True Resurrection*, *Reincarnate*, *Revivify* and *Clone*. Do they still exist, or do they work differently?
- **Death saving throws,** the Devout's *Steadfast Faith*, and the 0 Hit Points rules.
- **Monsters and NPCs:** do enemies phase too?
- **Factions:** how each faction treats newcomers. The Undertow has its "opportunity".
- **Character creation:** a starting character who has already phased, as a backstory option.
- **The Unphased:** the Long Rest trait swap, and how settling ends it.
- **Exhaustion and conditions,** if the penalty uses them.
- **Myth and religion:** phasing as rebirth; how the faiths of each dragon view it.
- **Life span and ageing.**

## 4. Levers already proposed (from the setting bible)

- **a.** The result is set by the event or rolled, not chosen, with *Reincarnate* as a model.
- **b.** A fading penalty, like *Raise Dead*'s, or Exhaustion.
- **c.** A cost in memories, relationships or proficiencies.
- **d.** Phasing only at the DM's discretion or at death's door.
- **e.** *(Claude, 2026-10-30)* Traits are kept, but the form's skill and tool choices reset.
- **f.** *(Claude, 2026-10-30)* Phasing takes time: about a week of Long Rests in a cocoon-like state.
