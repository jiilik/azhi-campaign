# Brainstorm: Phasing (and Mortality)

**Status: PROPOSED** (opened 2026-10-30). This is the working record for the phasing rules. Its final home is probably Character Origins ("Settling and Phasing"), with spillover into other chapters (§3). Nothing here is canon until Troy approves it.

## 1. What's already canon

- **Born Unphased:** all dragonborn are born polychromatic (Unphased). They **settle** into a form as they mature, and what triggers settling is left to the player's backstory.
- **Phasing:** something dramatic, traumatic or deadly can make a dragonborn **phase** from one form into another.
  - They keep parts of their former personality and most of their memories.
  - Rebirth runs deep, echoing the myth: everything Azhi devoured "springs forth anew".
  - Phasing **carries a penalty**, so it can't be used for advantage.
- **A New Society:** a phased dragonborn must find a place in their new colour. They stay in their old role, or start again at the bottom.
  - A newly phased Indigo has no name and no pearls, and is soon approached with an opportunity.
- **No Return:** once settled, there's no return to Unphased, except rarely, at the DM's discretion.
- **Languages** don't change when a dragonborn phases.
- **Gold and Silver** aren't easy to reach. They're backburnered for the DM material.
- *(Troy, 2026-10-30)* Since Azhi died and was reborn as a world, the game might remove mortality altogether, after a fashion. **Life span** was taken out of Character Origins to be settled here.

## 2. Open questions

1. **What is phasing, in game terms?** Is it an event the DM triggers, a player's choice, a consequence of death, or some mix?
2. **The trigger:** what counts as "dramatic, traumatic or deadly"? Does dropping to 0 Hit Points count? Does death?
3. **Death and mortality:** does phasing replace death, wholly or partly? Is there still permanent death?
4. **The new form:** is it chosen, rolled, or set by the event? Do neighbours on the wheel matter?
5. **What carries over:**
   - Personality traits (probably kept, since "parts of their former personality" survive).
   - Class levels, proficiencies, equipment, memories and relationships.
6. **The penalty:** what stops a player phasing on purpose to optimize?
7. **First settling:** is it phasing, with the penalty, or free?
8. **Age:** does phasing reset age or appearance?
9. **Frequency:** is there a limit on how often a dragonborn can phase?

## 3. Spillover to check

- **Spells:** *Raise Dead*, *Resurrection*, *True Resurrection*, *Reincarnate*, *Revivify* and *Clone*. Do they still exist, or do they work differently?
- **Death saving throws,** the Devout's *Steadfast Faith*, and the 0 Hit Points rules.
- **Monsters and NPCs:** do enemies phase too?
- **Factions:** how each faction treats newcomers. The Undertow has its "opportunity".
- **Character creation:** a starting character who has already phased, as a backstory option.
- **The Unphased:** the Long Rest trait swap, and how settling ends it.
- **Exhaustion and conditions,** if the penalty uses them.
- **Myth and religion:** phasing as rebirth; how the faiths of each dragon view it.
- **Life span and ageing.**

## 4. Levers already proposed (from the setting bible)

- **a.** The result is set by the event or rolled, not chosen, with *Reincarnate* as a model.
- **b.** A fading penalty, like *Raise Dead*'s, or Exhaustion.
- **c.** A cost in memories, relationships or proficiencies.
- **d.** Phasing only at the DM's discretion or at death's door.
- **e.** *(Claude, 2026-10-30)* Traits are kept, but the form's skill and tool choices reset.
- **f.** *(Claude, 2026-10-30)* Phasing takes time: about a week of Long Rests in a cocoon-like state.

## 5. Session notes

### 2026-10-30, evening (text)

**Troy's direction:**
- Make it an interesting system, with spells, effects, modifications and counters.
- There must still be **risk**. Phasing on death happens only under specific circumstances, such as a ritual performed on the corpse at the time of natural death.
- There should be **spells that force phasing** on the unwilling, such as *Power Word: Phase*.
- Fold the **resurrection and reincarnation spells** into the system.
- Phasing has **penalties and limits**. For example, you must **keep one personality trait**, linking your old self to your new self.
- Give a way to **swap a personality trait without a full phase**.

**The SRD 5.2.1 revival spells (checked against `srd/07-spells`):**

| Spell | Level, cost | Window | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Revivify* | 3, diamond 300 GP | dead 1 minute | none; not old age |
| *Raise Dead* | 5, diamond 500 GP | 10 days | −4 to D20 Tests, −1 less per Long Rest; not Undead; fails without the head |
| *Reincarnate* | 5 (druid), oils 1,000 GP | 10 days | new body, species rolled on a d10; keeps its capabilities, swaps species traits; no D20 penalty |
| *Resurrection* | 7, diamond 1,000 GP | a century | −4 to D20 Tests as *Raise Dead*; not old age; taxes the caster after 365 days |
| *True Resurrection* | 9, diamonds 25,000 GP | 200 years | none; not old age; can make a new body |
| *Clone* | 8, 3,000 GP in all | prepared ahead | soul moves to the clone; no penalty |
| *Gentle Repose* | 2, 2 CP | | pauses the clock on revival windows |

Note: **every revival spell excludes death by old age.** That's a natural gap for phasing to fill.

**Claude's proposal (PROPOSED):**

1. **Two kinds of death, two answers.**
   - **Violent death** (Hit Points, spells, poison) works as the SRD does: you're dead, and only revival magic brings you back, *as yourself, in the same form*.
   - **Natural death** (old age, the end of a life) is where phasing lives: *mortality, after a fashion*. With the right rite, a dragonborn at the end of their life is reborn in a new form instead of dying.
   - So the risk stays: adventurers can still die for good.
2. **The Rite of Rebirth** *(placeholder name)*: a ritual performed over a dragonborn at the moment of natural death, or within a short window after.
   - Done right, they **phase** instead of dying.
   - Its cost, who can perform it, and what happens if it fails or is interrupted are open (§6). This also gives the faiths of each dragon something to *do*: priests who keep the rite.
3. **Phasing rules, wherever phasing comes from:**
   - **Keep one trait** *(Troy)*: you keep at least one personality trait, which links old self to new. You may keep both.
   - **The new form** isn't freely chosen. It's set by the circumstances, by the DM, or rolled (see 4).
   - **Phase shock:** the new body is an ordeal. You take a −4 penalty to D20 Tests, reduced by 1 each Long Rest, reusing *Raise Dead*'s penalty.
   - **What carries over:** class levels, memories and languages carry over. Form features don't.
   - **A New Society:** already canon.
4. ***Reincarnate* becomes the phasing spell.** Its d10 table becomes the wheel:
   - 1–9: Red, Brown, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Black, Grey, White;
   - 10: the DM's choice, or roll again.

   It works on violent death too, which makes it the one spell that brings you back *changed*. The keep-one-trait rule applies.
5. **_Power Word: Phase_** *(new spell)*: force an unwilling creature to phase on the spot. It's modelled on *Power Word Kill*: it works if the target is at or under a Hit Point threshold, with no save.
   - The new form is rolled on the *Reincarnate* table, the target suffers phase shock, and it keeps one trait of the caster's choice?
   - Level 7 or 8, and the threshold, are to be balanced. It's a terrifying thing for an enemy to do, and a story hook.
6. **Counters and modifiers** *(ideas)*:
   - *Gentle Repose* extends the rite's window, as it already does for revival.
   - **A ward** (new, or a use of *Death Ward*?) stops forced phasing.
   - **_Anchor_** *(a new low-level spell, or a ritual)* lets a willing dragonborn **choose** the form of their next phase.
   - *Clone*: the soul moves to a clone of the *same* form, a way to dodge phasing.
   - *True Resurrection* restores even a phased creature to its original form.
7. **Swapping a trait without phasing** *(ideas)*:
   - **On level-up:** replace one personality trait, keeping the ability score rules. It's simple, and like the 2024 rules' cantrip and spell swaps.
   - **Through downtime,** such as a period of soul-searching or a life-changing experience, at the DM's discretion.
   - **By a spell,** *Change of Heart*, at a low level.
8. **Settling vs phasing:** a young dragonborn's first settling isn't a phase. There's no penalty, and the player chooses the form, as now.

**Troy, follow-up (2026-10-30):**
- Phasing also happens under **extreme stress or duress**. The character goes **catatonic** and emerges in a new form.
- **The tipping point varies:** a shipwreck, a failed performance where the crowd throws rotten fruit, a near-death experience.
- **Death-triggered phasing is just a particularly dramatic case** of the same thing.

**Claude's reshaping of the proposal (PROPOSED):**
- **One mechanism, many triggers.** A dragonborn phases when they reach a **breaking point**. Death is the most extreme breaking point, not a separate system.
  - This replaces proposal 1's split between violent and natural death as the *trigger*. The rite (proposal 2) becomes the way to **catch a dying dragonborn's breaking point** before death takes them.
- **The catatonia:** when a dragonborn breaks, they fall **catatonic** (Incapacitated, unresponsive) for a while, perhaps a few days. Then they emerge in their new form with phase shock.
  - The catatonia itself is the risk: helpless in a shipwreck, and someone has to carry you.
- **How a breaking point is reached** *(options)*:
  - **a. The DM calls it,** at a story moment, ideally with the player's agreement. Simple, but no dice.
  - **b. A save:** when something devastating happens, the DM calls for a Wisdom or Charisma save, with the DC set by how severe it is. On a failure you break.
    - Gives counters a place: Advantage against it from the Rock, or a ward.
  - **c. Through Exhaustion:** reaching **Exhaustion level 6** means you break and phase instead of dying.
    - Ties duress to an existing SRD condition. Starvation, exposure, despair or ordeals stack Exhaustion until the mind gives way.
  - **d. The player can choose to break,** at any breaking-point moment. For a player who wants a change of form, it's a story beat.
- **Death as the extreme case:** dropping to 0 Hit Points and failing death saves is the ultimate breaking point. Without the rite, or *Reincarnate*, it's still true death, so the risk survives.

### 6. Questions this raises

- **The rite:** who can perform it, what it costs, and how long the window lasts.
- **Failure:** what happens if the rite fails, or nobody performs it. Is that true death?
- **Timing:** does a phased elder return as an adult in the new form, or young again? This touches life span.
- **Limits:** is there a limit on how many lives a dragonborn can have?
- **Death by other causes:** could a violently killed dragonborn whose body is *also* given the rite phase? Or is the rite only for natural death?
- **Unphased:** what about an Unphased dragonborn who dies?
- **Forced phasing:** should *Power Word: Phase* let the victim keep the trait of their choice, or the caster's?
