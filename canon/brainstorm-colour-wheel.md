# Brainstorm: Rethinking the Colour Wheel

**Status: brainstorming only (PROPOSED).** The current wheel (five colours, five hybrids) is canon (`canon/decisions.md`, 2026-10-21) and stays so until Troy approves a change.

Opened 2026-10-23, from the discussion about naming the hybrids.

## 1. The idea *(Troy)*

- The wheel doesn't have to space the five primaries evenly. Some can be **squashed together with no hybrid between them**, for example Blue next to Green. That frees room elsewhere for a new colour, such as **purple** (or yellow).
- At a minimum, keep **the five original colours**.
- Hybrids to keep: **brown** and **grey**. They're easy to name and to picture elementally. Dark red and light blue are hard to name and conceptualize.
- Reorder into **rainbow order**: black next to purple, then grey, then white, then red.
- *(Troy, clarified)* Colours blend **like paint, not light**, so Green + Red is brown. This is about how blends look, not how they're named.

## 2. A wheel in rainbow order *(Claude's sketch)*

Going around: **Red → Brown → Green → Blue → Purple → Black → Grey → White → back to Red.** That's 8 forms: the 5 colours plus Brown, Purple and Grey.

| Position | Form | Made from | Elemental sense (Claude) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Red | — | fire, mountains, industry |
| 2 | **Brown** | Red + Green | earth: ash-fed soil, farmers and woodcutters |
| 3 | Green | — | life |
| — | *(no hybrid)* | Green + Blue squashed together | — |
| 4 | Blue | — | water |
| 5 | **Purple / Indigo** | Blue + Black | the deep sea, ink and dye, mystery |
| 6 | Black | — | knowledge, ambition |
| 7 | **Grey** | Black + White | stone and ash, old records, the investigator |
| 8 | White | — | ice, empathy |
| — | *(no hybrid)* | White + Red squashed together | — |

- **Paint check:** Blue + Black in paint makes a deep navy, not purple, because true purple is Blue + Red. **Indigo** fits the paint rule and sits next to violet in rainbow order. It's also the classic dye for ink, which ties to Black's knowledge. Purple stays possible if the paint rule bends for this one blend.
- **Yellow** has no natural home. In paint, Red + Green makes brown, and yellow would look too much like Gold.

## 3. What it would change in canon *(Claude)*

1. **The elemental wheel.** The canon feeding cycle runs Blue → Green → Red → Black → White → Blue, and the forbidden blends are its checking (opposed) pairs. The rainbow order puts the colours in a different sequence (Red, Green, Blue, Black, White), so:
   - **Black + Blue** becomes a hybrid, but it's a canon opposed pair ("Black's engineering dams Blue").
   - **White + Red** sit side by side with no hybrid, but they're also a canon opposed pair ("Red melts White").
   - The feeding and checking stories would need rewriting for the new order.
2. **Hybrids removed:** Marsh (Blue + Green), Oxblood (Red + Black) and Glacier (White + Blue), with their signature features (Swamp Walker, Inventive Genius, Seasoned Traveller), their points and their ribbons.
3. **Hybrids renamed:** Soil becomes Brown and Slate becomes Grey (or new names). Their features could carry over.
4. **A new hybrid:** Purple or Indigo needs a design: signature, skills, abilities and ribbons.
5. **Knock-ons:**
   - the trait doc's suggested colours;
   - the SRD species file;
   - the Character Origins doc;
   - the colour wheel art;
   - the ability lists for hybrids.
   - **Fewer forms** (8 instead of 10, plus the Unphased) also helps the choice-overload check.

## 4. Alternative that keeps the elemental cycle *(Claude)*

Keep the canon order (Blue, Green, Red, Black, White). Keep only the hybrids whose colours are easy to name: **Brown** (Green + Red) and **Grey** (Black + White). Squash the other three pairs, so there are no hybrids for Blue + Green, Red + Black or White + Blue. That gives 7 forms and no rewrite of the cycle, but no room for purple.

## 4b. Mixing like light instead *(Troy asked, Claude's analysis)*

| Blend | Paint | Light |
|---|---|---|
| Red + Green | brown | **yellow** |
| Green + Blue | teal | cyan |
| Blue + Red | purple | magenta (purple) |
| anything + Black | darker | **no change**: black adds no light |
| Black + White | grey | **white** (grey only if averaged) |

- In light, **Black and White aren't hues**; they're brightness, the absence of light and all of it. They fall off a light wheel. That wheel is Red, Yellow, Green, Cyan, Blue, Magenta.
- Light gives purple honestly, but it loses **brown and grey**, the two hybrids Troy wants to keep. It also makes **yellow**, which would look too much like Gold.
- *(Claude)* One idea it suggests: a hue wheel (Red, Green, Blue and their blends), with **Black and White as a separate dark–light axis** across it. Thematically, light belongs to Gold and Silver, the sun and moon.

## 4c. Yellow and Grey as earthly Gold and Silver *(Troy)*

- *(Troy)* A yellow close to Gold is fine. Grey is already close to Silver. These could be the **muted, unascended** forms: the **closest earthly equivalents of the heavenly dragons**.
- *(Claude)* That gives the wheel a pair of mortal echoes of the sun and moon:
  - **Yellow/Ochre ↔ Gold** (the sun);
  - **Grey ↔ Silver** (the moon).
- *(Claude)* **Ochre** may solve brown versus yellow. It's an earth pigment, the yellow-brown of clay and old cave paintings: a muted gold. It fits Red + Green whether you think of the mix as paint (brown) or light (yellow). It also keeps the earthy, farming feel of the old Soil hybrid.
- *(Claude)* **It could answer an OPEN question** in the setting bible: whether Gold and Silver can ever be reached by phasing. Ochre and Grey could be the rare stepping stones toward them, though still not an easy player path, as canon requires.

## 4d. A ten-slot wheel (2026-10-23, late evening)

- *(Troy)* **Ignore the opposed pairs** for now. There are **ten slots**: five primaries, plus the two earthly echoes of the heavenly dragons, which sit naturally between adjacent colours.
  - Troy's sketch: **red, ochre, green, blue, indigo, black, grey, white** (8).
- *(Claude)* Spelling: **grey** is the Canadian spelling, and the repo already uses it.
- *(Claude)* English has **eleven basic colour terms** (Berlin and Kay's research on colour words): black, white, red, green, yellow, blue, brown, purple, pink, orange and grey. The sketch covers seven of them; ochre spans yellow and brown, and indigo stands in for purple. Missing are **pink** and **orange**, plus yellow and brown as separate colours.
- *(Claude)* **Splitting ochre into Brown and Yellow, and adding Pink, makes exactly ten:**

| # | Form | Sits between | Sense (Claude) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Red | — | fire, mountains, industry |
| 2 | **Brown** | Red and Yellow | earth and soil (brown is a darkened orange, so it sits by Red) |
| 3 | **Yellow** | Brown and Green | the earthly echo of Gold, the sun |
| 4 | Green | — | life |
| — | *(no hybrid)* | Green and Blue squashed together | |
| 5 | Blue | — | water |
| 6 | **Indigo** | Blue and Black | the deep sea, ink and dye |
| 7 | Black | — | knowledge, ambition |
| 8 | **Grey** | Black and White | the earthly echo of Silver, the moon |
| 9 | White | — | ice, empathy |
| 10 | **Pink** | White and Red | White + Red in paint; dawn, blossom, warmth (conceptually the weakest) |

- **Orange** is the alternative for slot 10. It's more vivid than pink but crowds Brown and Yellow, and Orange would need to replace Brown or Pink.
- **Mixing:** with the opposed pairs set aside, the simplest rule is **neighbours blend, and each blend is named for how it looks**. Yellow and Grey are special as the earthly echoes of Gold and Silver, so they don't need to follow paint physics exactly.

## 4e. Nine forms *(Troy)*

*(Troy)* **Drop both Pink and Orange; use nine forms.**

**Red → Brown → Yellow → Green → Blue → Indigo → Black → Grey → White → back to Red**

| Gap | Hybrid(s) |
|---|---|
| Red and Green | **Brown** (by Red) and **Yellow** (by Green, the earthly echo of Gold) |
| Green and Blue | none (squashed together) |
| Blue and Black | **Indigo** |
| Black and White | **Grey** (the earthly echo of Silver) |
| White and Red | none (squashed together) |

- That's 5 colours and 4 hybrids, which with the Unphased makes **10 options at character creation**.
- **Compared with current canon:**
  - **Retired:** Marsh, Oxblood, Glacier.
  - **Carried over:** Soil becomes Brown; Slate becomes Grey.
  - **New:** Yellow, Indigo.
- *(Claude)* **Open: Brown and Yellow share the Red–Green gap.** Both have Red and Green as parents. One reading: **Brown** is Red and Green mixed like paint, the earth; **Yellow** is the same two mixed like light, the sun's earthly echo.

  - Its flavour (great travellers, envoys, caravan masters) doesn't yet match Indigo's sense (the deep sea, ink and dye).

## 4g. Indigo, adapted from Oxblood with some of Glacier's travel *(Troy's direction, Claude's draft)*

*(Troy)* Indigo takes **Oxblood's inventor**, plus **some of Glacier's travel sense**. They don't just invent; they **travel to learn**.

> #### Indigo (Blue + Black)
>
> _Indigo dragonborn are travelling inventors and seekers. They sail and walk the length of the world to learn how others build, trade notes in every port, and come home with journals full of ideas, written in their own indigo ink, and a few contraptions to show for them._
>
> **Creature Type:** Humanoid
> **Size:** Medium
> **Speed:** 30 feet
> **Ability Score Increase:** Intelligence or Dexterity
>
> As an Indigo dragonborn, you have these special traits.
>
> _Inventive Genius._ Once per Long Rest, you can rig up a contraption that replicates one of the following level 1 spells, chosen when you make it: _Alarm_, _Feather Fall_ or _Grease_. _(OPEN: how long a contraption takes to build, how it's triggered and what sets its save DC. The spell list will be revisited when spells are reviewed.)_
>
> _Well Travelled._ When you arrive in a settlement, you know its local customs, who's in charge, and a rumour or two.
>
> _Careful Senses._ If you spend 10 minutes examining an object, substance or device with your senses, you have Advantage on checks to work out what it is or how it works.
>
> _Indigo Training._ You have proficiency in two of the following skills of your choice: Arcana, History, Insight or Survival. You also have proficiency with one of the following tools of your choice: Alchemist's Supplies, Cartographer's Tools or Tinker's Tools.
>
> _Ribbons._ Careful Senses counts as one of your ribbons. _Indigo Ink:_ you can make serviceable ink or dye from almost anything to hand.

**What changed and why *(Claude)*:**

- **Kept from Oxblood:**
  - Inventive Genius, word for word.
  - Careful Senses, still counting as a ribbon.
  - Tinker's Tools or Alchemist's Supplies, which keeps the firearms route.
  - Arcana and History.
- **Kept from Glacier:** *Heard It on the Road*, the settlement knowledge, now called **Well Travelled**. Mounts and vehicles are dropped.
- **Changed:**
  - **Intimidation** is dropped. It came from the Red side, which Indigo doesn't have. **Insight** and **Survival** (from Glacier's list) come in for the traveller.
  - **Cartographer's Tools** is added to the tool choice, for mapping the journey.
- **Abilities:** Intelligence or Dexterity. Black gives Intelligence; Blue gives Dexterity, the steady hands of a tinkerer. Charisma was the other option (both parents offer it), but the inventor reads better with Dexterity.
- **New ribbon:** *Indigo Ink* ties the form to dye and ink.
- **Points:** about 17 (Inventive Genius about 7, Well Travelled about 1.5, Careful Senses 1, two skills 3, a tool 1, the +1 4). That's above the old hybrid range of about 13–16. Canon puts bichromes below the primaries (17–18.5), so to trim, take **one skill instead of two**, for about 15.5.

## 4i. Indigo: the deep lurkers *(Troy's direction, Claude's draft)*

- *(Troy)* **Override: Indigo may have Darkvision.** This is an exception to the canon rule "Darkvision belongs to Black alone". Apply it to canon (and to design review check 7) when the wheel rework is adopted.
- *(Troy)* **Drop the tinkerer material entirely.** Lean into the lurker in the deeps.
- *(Troy)* **Charisma is the only ability choice.**
- *(Troy)* The vibe:
  - Between Black and Blue: secretive and furtive.
  - A shadow civilization that stands apart because others can't reach it.
  - The keepers of dark secrets, who trade in danger.
  - A mystique like the vampire tropes (flavour only, since there are no other sentient species).
  - Connections and influence in the port towns are the currency of their clout at home.
  - They pull levers no one else even knows exist.

## 4j. The secret society beneath the surface *(Troy)*

- *(Troy)* Without the villainy, Indigo feels like **the unknowable secret society living just beneath the surface, as in John Wick**.
- *(Claude)* Ideas borrowed from that feel. These are inspiration only; don't use the films' names.
  - **Neutral houses:** quiet Indigo-run houses in the port towns, where business is done and violence is forbidden. Anyone may shelter there, if they keep the rules.
  - **Debts as currency:** favours owed and repaid, marked with a token such as a black pearl from the deeps. A debt is never forgotten, and it's always collected.
  - **Etiquette:** strict, unwritten rules that outsiders only half understand.
- *(Claude)* **A firmer Undertow**, using debt tokens so it depends less on the DM: you begin with a number of favours (pearls) equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and spend one to call on the network for a small service in a port. You earn more through play, and pearls can be traded, given or owed.

## 4k. Pearls, debts and being cast out (voice session, 2026-10-24)

### Currency or resource?

- *(Troy)* A pearl can't be both a **shadow currency** and a **refreshing player resource**. A currency shouldn't refresh; you get it only by doing something and being rewarded, like the soul coins of classic D&D lore. A daily resource is more like Heroic Inspiration or Bardic Inspiration.
- *(Claude)* One way to split it: the species feature is a plain once-per-rest call on the network, with no pearls involved, and the pearls become a setting currency anyone can earn, trade or owe. Indigo are the people who know best how to spend them.

### Troy's version: borrowing against the deep

- *(Troy)* An Indigo can call on the network **once per day**. If they have no pearl, they **go into debt**: a pearl owed to someone, which can be **called in later**, or settled when someone else gives them a pearl.
- *(Claude, on limits)* Some limit is needed, or a player could borrow forever.
- *(Troy)* **The society has debt collectors.** Go too deep and shadowy figures start turning up with demands.
- *(Troy)* **A called-in debt can't be refused.** Refusing means being **cast out of the society**, or being forced to **phase**.
- *(Claude's layout)*
  - **Small debts,** up to your Proficiency Bonus, are normal business.
  - **Deeper,** and the collectors come calling.
  - **Refusing** a called debt means you're cast out and lose the network.
  - Forced phasing is fascinating, but harsh: a whole form lost over one refusal. Being cast out while still Indigo may be sting enough.

### Starting cast out

- *(Troy)* Being cast out can combine with the personality traits. For example, a renegade character (the Unbound) could **start the game already cast out**. The DM should then ask **why** as part of the backstory, which gives the campaign hooks. If it happens during play instead, everyone knows exactly why.
- *(Claude)* A cast-out Indigo loses Undertow and so falls a few points short. One balance: they know the society from the outside, so they can spot its agents and dodge its collectors.

### Why it matters

- *(Troy)* Needs tinkering to make sure it's **fun to play**. But it gives Indigo a unique flavour: **a form that comes with a faction**, which the others may not have.
- **Open (Claude):** should any other form get a lighter faction of its own, or should that stay Indigo's alone?

### Next

- *(Troy)* Define the forms not yet designed, **starting with Yellow**. Brown and Grey are already defined (from Soil and Slate), so skip them.

## 4l. Societies, knowledge colours, Yellow and a Green rework (voice session, 2026-10-24)

### Phasing as emigration

- *(Troy)* A dragonborn who phases must be **taken in by their new society**. Either they stay in the same role under a new colour, or they seek out others like them and **start fresh, perhaps at the bottom of the ladder**, in a new civilization or faction.
- *(Claude)* How welcoming that is could differ by colour: some take newcomers in, others make them prove themselves.
- *(Troy)* **Phasing into Indigo** means arriving with **no reputation** in a reputation-driven society. *(Claude)* With no pearls and no name, borrowing is the only way in, so a newly phased Indigo starts their new life in debt.

### Societies

- *(Troy)* **Blue** are trade-oriented and welcome everyone, so the port cities they run are **cosmopolitan, diverse and fun places to set campaigns**. The setting's equivalent of Baldur's Gate would be a Blue city.
- *(Troy)* **White** live in **monasteries hidden in the mountains**. White characters turn up anywhere, but rarely in large numbers outside the monasteries.
- *(Claude)* Indigo's neutral houses would sit inside the Blue ports.

### The wheel as colour order, not opposition

- *(Troy)* **Worry less about adjacency and opposition.** The wheel is a colour order, and **rule of cool** applies for now. *(Claude)* This sets aside the canon checking pairs as structure. The current order, Red, Brown, Yellow, Green, stays, even though by role Yellow sits nearer Red and Brown nearer Green.

### Red, Yellow, Brown and Green: industry and nature

- *(Troy)* **Red** are large, tool-oriented **smiths** and probably the **miners**.
- *(Troy)* The alchemist and inventor role goes to **Yellow** rather than Red: a bright flame burns more yellow than red, and a gunpowder flash is yellow-white.
  - *(Claude)* Red keeps the forge (steady heat, hammered metal); Yellow takes the flash (alchemy, gunpowder, invention). That gives the **firearms route** a home again.
- *(Troy)* Yellow and Brown sit between Red and Green, almost like industrialists:
  - **Red** mines.
  - **Yellow** turns the ore into inventions.
  - **Brown** farms.
  - **Green**, the environmentalists, resist the farming, which turns natural land into harvested land. They may resist inventions that run counter to their goals, too.
  - *(Claude)* That's a land-use dispute, not prejudice.

### Yellow, the open-source inventors

- *(Troy)* Yellow are **freewheeling and open-source**: knowledge should be shared publicly. "Never let your candle be dimmed." *(Claude)* Lighting someone else's candle costs you nothing.
- *(Troy)* Yellow have **poor business sense** about their inventions and are bad at the whole intellectual-property thing.
  - **Blue and Yellow** make a natural pair: the business brains and the inventor brains working in tandem.
  - *(Claude)* Yellow invents it, Blue sells it, and Yellow wonders where the money went.
- *(Troy)* Green aren't necessarily upset with Yellow, because they don't think Yellow is trying to pave over the forest. Yellow try to invent things that **suit other people's needs**, even things to help preserve the forest.
  - *(Claude)* Green see Yellow as naive, not as an enemy. Their real quarrel is with whoever turns the inventions into profit and paved fields.

### The five knowledge colours

| Colour | Stance on knowledge |
|---|---|
| Black | **hoards** it and keeps it secret |
| Indigo | **trades** it as secrets |
| Yellow | **gives it away**, open source |
| White | **teaches** it as wisdom (clerics, monasteries); agrees or clashes with Yellow depending on the topic, for example over dangerous knowledge |
| Grey | **uncovers** it (Gandalf, investigators); finds Yellow's free sharing troublesome |

- *(Troy)* The joke that sums it up: at a crime scene, "another Yellow dragonborn blowing something up they don't understand."
  - *(Claude)* A possible Yellow ribbon: people assume any explosion nearby was your fault.
- *(Claude)* **Watch:** five of nine colours are knowledge-oriented. Their motivations differ, but their skills could blur.

### Yellow's features

- A sketch by Claude was **rejected by Troy**: fire resistance and a dazzling flash, inventor's tools, and an "open book" ribbon. Start Yellow's features fresh.

### Green rework *(Troy)*

- *(Troy)* Lean into **Green's Dexterity side** (it already has a Climb Speed) instead of **Regeneration**. Give it a **prehensile tail**.
- *(Troy)* Minor combat uses are fine, provided that isn't all it does, like the natural weapons of the non-SRD Tabaxi.
- *(Claude)* A version written fresh (Tabaxi aren't in the SRD):
  - the tail can hold a light object or let you hang from a branch, keeping your hands free while you climb;
  - it can make a **1d6 bludgeoning** tail swipe, or perhaps a trip or shove.
- *(Claude, checked against the repo)* Green today:

| Feature | Points |
|---|---|
| Regeneration | 8 |
| Climb Speed | 2 |
| Nature | 1.5 |
| Survival | 1.5 |
| Herbalism or Poisoner's Kit | 1 |
| +1 to Constitution | 4 |
| **Total** | **18** |

  - The tail is worth perhaps **3–4**, so swapping out Regeneration leaves **about 4–5 points to fill**.
  - The swap also drops Green's design intent as the burst tank who takes a beating, along with the flavour line "Green dragonborn take a beating and keep going."
  - *(Troy)* **Green's +1 becomes Dexterity or Constitution.** That gives Dexterity a second home on the wheel (it was Blue only) and keeps Constitution's two (Green, Brown).
