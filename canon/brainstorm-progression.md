# Brainstorm: A Progression System Built for Phasing (Option D)

**Status: EXPLORING** (opened 2026-11-05). This file explores replacing classes with trees. Nothing is decided: classes are not yet dropped. Troy's statements are marked *(Troy)*; everything else is Claude's and PROPOSED.

**Background.** Troy asked whether phasing should let a level 12 cleric become a level 12 wizard, then whether classes should go entirely, with progression built around phasing from the outset. Claude offered four options (`canon/brainstorm-extended-traits.md` §3a). Troy chose to explore option D, "everything is trees".

## 1. Troy's design (2026-11-05)

- **Two trees.**
  - **The experience tree** is what you learn and remember. **When you phase, you keep it.**
  - **The phasing tree** is the feat-like progression: the extended traits, anchored to your form and personality traits. **When you phase, you reset on it**, but keep your experience tree.
- **Six entry nodes** on the experience tree, one for each ability. Each has an **entry threshold**, for example Dexterity 13 to start on the Dexterity tree.
- **The entry node is a starter kit.** Starting with Dexterity gives a mix of benefits like a Dexterity-focused class (a monk or ranger): an appropriate Hit Die, some proficiencies, and so on.
- **Then it branches.** The tree **doesn't have to be linear**.
- **Five nodes from the entry** is a significant boost, like a class gets at level 5: Extra Attack, level 3 spells and so on.
- **Hit Die boosts and proficiency boosts** come along the way.
- **On level-up:** choose one node from each tree, **or maybe two from any**. The combinations should make super fun builds.
- **Nature versus learning:** some features, such as the barbarian's **Rage**, feel like a personality trait that you can gain or lose by phasing. Others, such as **Sneak Attack**, are learned and remembered.

### Shallow and deep *(Troy, 2026-11-05)*

- **The phasing tree is powerful but shallow.** When you phase, you lose it: that loss is **the true phasing penalty**.
- **The experience tree is deep,** and you keep your learned abilities when you phase.
- **After a phase,** you can keep pursuing the same part of the experience tree, or start fresh on another section of it.
- **Over a long life,** a character who survives several phases could be deep in many parts of the experience tree: like being ridiculously multiclassed beyond level 20. Most campaigns don't last that long anyway.

**Claude's notes** *(PROPOSED)*:
- **Shallow means wide.** With one phasing node per level, a level 20 character holds 20 phasing nodes. A tree only about five tiers deep (the extended-traits tech tree) has to be wide enough to hold them: many strong nodes near the root.
- **"Beyond level 20" implies the levels come back.** If phasing resets the phasing tree and you then re-earn it level by level, each of those levels also brings a new experience node, so the experience tree can grow past 20 nodes. Whether phasing resets your level is the next question.

## 1a. Three trees (voice session, driving, 2026-11-06)

Written up from the voice transcript. One line in the transcript was attributed to Troy but **not said by him** ("Sounds like a personal challenge"), so it is left out, and Claude's reply to it is marked as Claude's.

**This revises §1 (two trees) and the 2026-11-05 decisions on the per-level split and the phasing reset.** Where they conflict, this section is newer; the conflicts are listed at the end.

### Three trees *(Troy)*

- **Split three ways, not two**, because three different things are going on:
  1. **Form:** your colour of dragonborn, and how far along the dragonborn progression you are. It's the biggest outward change on phasing, and sets a lot of physical parameters.
  2. **Personality:** the personality traits, which are lost or kept on phasing.
  3. **Learned skills:** the six ability entry points from 2026-11-05. **Kept on phasing:** you don't lose your memories. Your personality might shift, but your skills and experience remain.
- **Dragons who phase stay full dragons.** For example, the Crimson phasing to Brown, or the elder Black phasing to Indigo. Neither goes back to being a dragonborn or anything lesser, and neither loses power or ability.

### What phasing does to each tree *(Troy)*

- **Form: points come back.** Whatever you had invested in the form tree is available to reinvest after the phase. Seven points in the Green branch, phased to White: reinvest them in White, and you may end up further along the form than a beginner. This is **gameable, on purpose**.
- **Personality: the real penalty.** Everyone starts with two traits. On a phase you **keep one**. Anything that depended on the lost trait is **simply lost**, and anything that depended on the kept trait is **retained**. You partly keep your personality. No points come back, so it isn't gameable: nobody can force a phase just to switch branches. This **offsets the advantage of the form tree**.
- **Learned skills: all kept.**

### The personality tree: shallow and converging *(Troy, 2026-11-06, text)*

- **Pretty shallow: no more than four levels deep.** A new character already has two traits, at the entry points.
- The nodes are **short trait trees, more like mini feats**.
- **Maximum collected:** **8** for a phased character (two roots × 4), or **12** for an Unphased one (three roots × 4).
- This **assumes phasing is relatively common**.
- **Converging, not branching:** **24 → 16 → 12 → 8**, so there are **8 possible final traits**. That is **60 things to develop**, but you can only hold a few at a time.

*(Claude, PROPOSED or open)*:
- **Converging cuts the maximum.** If a character's two roots converge on the same node, they share it, and the maximum drops below 8. Is that a perk (an easy, natural pairing) or a trap? **OPEN (Troy, 2026-11-06):** neither; we'll devise a different graph shape that is neither perk nor trap.
- **A converged node after a phase:** if it's reachable from both the kept and the lost trait, it should stay, since the kept trait still supports it.
- **The Unphased swap one trait each Long Rest** (CANON, 2026-10-21). With trait trees, does a swap also drop the nodes built on the swapped trait?
- **Pick counts:** beyond the roots, a phased character has only 6 personality picks before the tree is full (an Unphased one 9). After that, points must go to the other trees until a phase frees room.

### Points per advance *(parked)*

- **Parked by Troy** until the tree depths are known: trees may differ in depth, and a point in each tree per advance could max out one tree before another.
- Troy's concern: a free split of points lets a player ignore the personality tree and so dodge the phasing penalty.
- Troy floated **three points per level-up**, with deeper form rings costing more (below).
- *(Claude, PROPOSED, not discussed by Troy)* Options: one point in each tree; or one in personality every time plus one free; or a free split with at least one point into personality each level.

### The learned-skills tree: a hex grid *(Troy)*

**Renamed (Troy, 2026-11-07): the hex grid is the skills grid. Its centre tile is empty**, with the six ability hexes around it. Ring 1 is the first to populate (voice session, 2026-11-07).

- **Start here** at the centre hex, surrounded by **one hex per ability**.
- **Physical abilities on one side, mental on the other**, so neighbours make sense. **Wisdom and Intelligence must be adjacent**, and so must **Constitution and Strength**.
- **Ring order: Charisma, Dexterity, Strength, Constitution, Wisdom, Intelligence**, then back to Charisma. Proposed by Claude; Troy: "seems fine from a game design perspective", with lots of fantasy to play in. Fantasies the ring misses can come from the other two trees.
  - The seams: Charisma–Dexterity (swashbucklers, bards) and Constitution–Wisdom (monks, shamans).
- **Concerns** *(Troy)*:
  - Two neighbours each may be restrictive.
  - Crossing sideways too quickly could let a character take five combat nodes, then slip into a magical ability with higher-level spells and none of the prerequisites. Troy doesn't want that.
- *(Claude, PROPOSED)* Crossing never counts as depth: a hybrid node can mix effects (fire on arrows), but spell power comes only from depth in a casting branch. Other routes across: a second entry node, or a table of hybrids.
- **Printing:** mostly pen and paper *(Troy)*. Six fully connected points can't be drawn flat without crossings. A computer version that reorders the layout came up as brainstorming only.
- **Size** *(Claude's arithmetic, Troy: "quite a lot… lots of design space")*: entry plus five more steps needs **six rings**; ring *n* holds 6*n* hexes; **127 hexes** in all, **21 per ability**.
  - **Ring 1 is special** *(Troy)*: the base class, with the multiclassing limits (full kit first, reduced after).
  - That leaves **120 regular nodes, 20 per ability**.

**Ring 1, part 3: what goes in each kit (voice, 2026-11-07)** *(Troy unless marked)*

*Balance:*
- **Every hex is worth about the same**, so a dip into another part of the grid grows a character as much as anything else.
- **A starting kit is a whole level 1.** Taking a kit later (multiclassing) gives a **reduced version worth about one regular hex**. **For now, a second kit gives everything**; it gets trimmed later.

*Two pools* (replacing "mana only"):
- **Stamina (SP)** fuels martial techniques; **mana (MP)** fuels spells. **Separate pools**, so going deep in the martial side and dipping one Mage tile doesn't give a huge casting pool. Stamina is the body's fuel and mana the mind's. Names from video games; abbreviations at most two characters, for the tiles.
- **A shared martial pool**, like 4th Edition: warlord commands, Battle Master-style manoeuvres and monk techniques all draw on SP.
- **Costs:** a martial technique costs **1 SP**; spells cost mana on the **2014 DMG spell points variant's prices, for now**: levels 1–9 cost 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 and 13. *(Claude: levels 1–5 match the SRD sorcerer's slot costs; levels 6–9 are only the variant's numbers, no text, and worth revisiting before publication. The nova limit is still open.)* *(Claude, voice: one technique is about half a level 1 spell, so a point of either pool is worth about the same.)*
- **Recovery:** **all on a Long Rest**. On a **Short Rest, each pool recovers points equal to your Proficiency Bonus**. This rule goes **in the rules text, not on a tile**, and must be written up. Better short-rest recovery can come as nodes later.
- **SP and MP go on line 3** of a tile, since they add up across tiles. **Symbols** *(Troy, 2026-11-07)*: ❤️ Hit Die, ⭐ stamina, 📜 mana.
- **At-wills** *(superseded 2026-11-07 by **techniques**, which can have a free use, a stamina use or both; see `canon/techniques.md`)*: free martial features that cost no stamina, the martial equivalent of cantrips. Named for 4th Edition, so players understand at once. **Designed later**, as part of the larger system.

### The form tree: the colour wheel *(Troy)*

- **The colour wheel**, not the hex grid.
- **The Unphased are at the centre** and **can't develop on the form tree until they phase**.
- **Starting Unphased:** three personality roots and none on the colour wheel. **On their first phase they drop from three traits to two**, to keep the penalty from getting too large. After that the normal keep-one rule applies.
- **Nine colours around the wheel** *(Troy; checked against the setting bible 2026-11-06: correct, the nine forms plus the Unphased in the centre)*.
- **Neighbours on the wheel don't matter.**
- **Each colour is a spoke** of blocks expanding through the rings. A ring may offer a choice or a fixed step: for Green, perhaps one of two, then one fixed option, then one of three.
- **The depth problem:** form points are reinvested after a phase, so a character with 20 points on the skill tree would need a form tree 20 deep: 20 rings, quite a lot of material.
- **Escalating costs** *(Troy)*: deeper form rings cost more points, perhaps on a **Fibonacci progression**, so each next feature gets harder to reach. **Progress here sacrifices progress in another tree.**
  - *(Claude's arithmetic)* Costs of 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 13 give running totals of 1, 2, 4, 7, 12, 20 and 33. Twenty points reaches **six rings**; even everything into form over a career tops out around **eight**.

## 2. Parts the SRD already has *(Claude)*

- **The threshold of 13** is the SRD's multiclassing prerequisite: "a score of at least 13 in the primary ability of the new class". An entry node is the same rule, applied to every character.
- **Spellcasting across trees:** the SRD's **Multiclass Spellcaster table** already turns combined caster levels into spell slots. Spellcasting nodes could count as caster levels on it, full or half, so "level 3 spells five nodes in" falls out of an existing table.
- **Hit points and proficiencies when multiclassing** are already solved in the SRD: Hit Dice by level, and reduced proficiencies for a second class. A second entry node could use the same rule.
- **The phasing tree's root already exists.** A character's form and two personality traits, from Character Origins, are its tier 0. The extended traits grow from them.
- **The class features** break down into a toolbox, like the feats. Done 2026-11-05: `canon/parts-bin.md` holds every class and feat part with a points score.

   - **Answered (Troy, 2026-11-05): only from nodes.** Levelling up means choosing one node from each tree, and **there are no other benefits to levelling up**. The level-up tables go with the classes. Even the concept of levels can be dropped, except to mean "gaining a node from each tree".
   - *(Claude)* Knock-ons to check: the d4 is new (the SRD's smallest Hit Die is d6); monster attack bonuses, save DCs and Challenge Ratings assume a Proficiency Bonus set by level (+2 to +6), so a character who skips the boosts falls behind on every roll.
3a. **What replaces "character level"** *(Troy, 2026-11-05)*: **scaling becomes nodes.** Cantrip upgrades and multiple attacks (Extra Attack and its successors) are things to acquire in the trees. **Encounter building** is to be checked against something like **the party's average total nodes**.
   - *(Claude)* Canon text that scales by character level will need a new measure, with Troy's approval: the Disciplined's *Muscle Memory* (character levels 5, 11 and 17), the Hearty's *Hardy* (HP per level), and anything else in Character Origins that counts levels. The SRD's XP thresholds also need a new meaning: XP per pair of nodes.
4. **A second entry node** *(Troy, 2026-11-05: **yes, with a reduced starter kit**, as the SRD's multiclassing rules reduce a second class's proficiencies)*: can you enter a second ability tree (the equivalent of multiclassing), and does its starter kit come at full or reduced strength?
5. **The Constitution tree** *(Troy, 2026-11-05: **a mix, branching, and likely recombinant**; Claude's options were the tank, the survivor and the vessel)*.
   - **Design principles for every tree** *(Troy)*:
     - **Each entry point has at least two initial branches.**
     - Branches can **recombine**.
     - **Balance depth:** important nodes on any branch take a similar number of steps to reach comparable milestones.
     - **Depth first ≈ the 5e power curve:** a player who heads straight for the milestones should progress much like a single-class character in the 2024 rules.
     - **Branching ≈ multiclassing:** slower on some fronts, but with interesting combination abilities.
     - **Very old characters** might survive to pursue depth on many paths, or a great deal of breadth.
   - *Original options:* no SRD class has Constitution as its primary ability, so its entry node is new design space. A tank, a survivor, or a vessel?
6. **Spellcasting ability** *(Troy, 2026-11-05: **option C, explore it**)*: a spellcasting branch can sit in any tree and uses that tree's ability. Claude's other options were A, the classes' abilities (Int arcane, Wis divine and primal, Cha innate), and B, set by the teaching dragon.
   - **Learned and natural magic in one character** *(Troy)*: learned magic, like the wizard's, on the experience tree, and natural magic on the phasing tree, both in the same character.
   - **Some magic may sit outside the trees** *(Troy)*. A warlock's pact is thematically more like **membership in a faction** than a skill or ability.
   - **No warlock equivalent on either tree, for now** *(Troy)*. That avoids disentangling Pact Magic from the lore, the biggest source of problems. The pact-as-faction idea is parked.
   - **A mana pool instead of spell slots** *(Troy, 2026-11-05, direction)*: eschew spell slots; gain mana through different branches. It keeps things simple. Troy recalls a variant like it in the 2014 DMG.
     - *(Claude)* The 2014 DMG did have a spell points variant, but it isn't in either SRD, so its text and tables can't be used. The SRD has an open basis instead: the sorcerer's **Creating Spell Slots** table prices slots in Sorcery Points (level 1: 2, level 2: 3, level 3: 5, level 4: 6, level 5: 7). Costs for levels 6–9 would be ours to set.
     - *(Claude)* The usual risk of a mana pool is the nova: spending everything on the highest-level spell again and again. A common fix is to allow each spell of level 6 or higher only once per Long Rest. To decide later.
   - **Spell lists** *(Troy, 2026-11-05)*: **a curated list per casting branch** (option A; the others were the teaching dragon's list and one shared list). But there must be **ways to short-circuit** it, like the bard's Magical Secrets.
     - *(Claude, PROPOSED)* Short-circuit nodes:
       - **Secrets nodes:** prepare spells from another branch's list. The SRD 5.2 bard's *Magical Secrets* (level 10) is the model; it's in the parts bin.
       - **Taught by a dragon:** learn spells from one of the Seven's lists in the spell origins. A natural place for the dragons' teaching.
       - **The spellbook:** an Intelligence branch that can copy any spell it finds written down, from scrolls or books, as the SRD wizard does from its own list.
       - **Where branches meet:** a node at a recombination point that merges the two branches' lists.
   - *Original question:* do arcane spells follow Intelligence, divine and primal Wisdom, and innate Charisma, as in the classes? Where do the dragons' teachings fit (`canon/brainstorm-spell-origins.md`)?
7. **The power curve** *(answered by the principles under 5: depth first tracks the 2024 power curve)*: keep the SRD's per-level power, so monsters' Challenge Ratings and encounter building still work.
8. **Presets** *(Troy, 2026-11-05): **no presets.***
   - It gives more flexibility in the design space, and room for fantasy tropes beyond the usual 12, such as a **shaman, warden, warlock or summoner**. *(Claude: the warlock trope can return as a design goal, even though no warlock branch exists for now.)*
   - **Schools and domains aren't limits**, though they might become branches of sorts.
   - **Examples** *(Troy)*:
     - Start in Wisdom, go down the **elemental** branch, pick up **fire** in it, and become a firebender-style monk.
     - Start in Dexterity, go down the **archer** branch, and pick up **fast reload** for multiple attacks.
     - **Cross-branch nodes:** if two branches are **adjacent** and the character has the prerequisites from both, they can reach a node such as **elemental arrows of fire**. Such a node can be a spell unlock, or a feature like a monk's.
   - *Original question:* keep the 12 classes as named paths through the trees, so a Player's Handbook player can still say "I'm a wizard"?
9. **Level 1** *(Troy, 2026-11-05)*: **one entry node plus the origin, at least on the experience side.** The entry node is the starter kit (proficiencies, a Hit Die, a signature feature), like a class's level 1. **The phasing side needs polish:** it has 33 ways in (24 traits and 9 forms). *(Claude)* A phased character holds three roots (a form and two traits); an Unphased one holds three traits and no form.
   - *Original question:* what does a new character have? Probably an entry node and the phasing tree's root (form and traits).
